The honest version

Your first hour,
minute by minute

The thing that stops most people isn't price and it isn't fitness. It's not knowing what they're walking into. So here it is, start to finish.

−10:00Before

You arrive early

Come ten minutes before class. Someone will meet you at the door, take your name, and show you where to put your things. You don't need to know anyone and you don't need to explain yourself.

00:00Start

Nobody makes you introduce yourself

No circle, no announcement, no being singled out as the new person. You line up at the back, you follow along, and that's it.

00:05Warm-up

Movement, at your own pace

A light warm-up to get moving. If something hurts or you need to stop, you stop. There is no punishment for sitting one out and nobody will comment on it.

00:15Technique

One thing, taught slowly

The coach demonstrates a single technique and breaks it into steps. You are then paired with someone experienced whose actual job is to help you get it right — not to beat you.

00:35Drilling

Practice with no resistance

You repeat the movement while your partner lets you work. This is where it starts to click. You will be bad at it. Everyone was.

00:50Rolling

You can sit this part out

Live sparring happens at the end. On your first night you are welcome to watch instead — plenty of people do, and no one thinks anything of it. When you do join in, you go with someone who knows how to control the pace.

01:00Finish

You leave knowing whether it's for you

No sales pitch at the door, no contract in your face. You've got two more free classes to decide. Most people come back the next night.

Before you ask

The questions
everyone has

Asked by almost every person who walks in. Nothing here is a silly question.

Do I need to be fit before I start?

No. Turning up is how you get fit — trying to get fit first is how people put it off for two years. You set your own pace in every class, you can stop whenever you need to, and nobody is watching you.

Will I be the oldest person there?

Almost certainly not. Our adult classes run from people in their late teens to people in their fifties, and a good share started in their thirties or forties with no background in anything. Jiu jitsu rewards technique over athleticism, which is exactly why it suits people who aren't twenty anymore.

What if I've never done any sport or martial art?

That's the most common starting point, not the unusual one. The first class is designed to be followed by someone who has never done it. You'll be shown one technique slowly and paired with someone experienced whose job is to help you.

Do I need to buy a gi?

Not for the trial. Turn up in shorts and a t-shirt. If you decide to keep training we'll point you at what to buy — and it's less than most people expect.

Is it safe? Will I get hurt?

Jiu jitsu is trained with control, not force. You tap, your partner stops — that's the whole system, and it's why people can train it hard for decades. Beginners drill without resistance and are never paired with someone who can't control themselves. Muay Thai beginners work pads and technique, not sparring.

What if I can't make it every week?

Come when you can. Classes aren't a course with a syllabus you fall behind on — every session stands on its own, and there are twenty-two a week to fit around your schedule.

Can I just watch a class first?

Yes. Message us, come in, sit down and watch. Plenty of people do that before their first session and it's often the thing that gets them on the mat.
